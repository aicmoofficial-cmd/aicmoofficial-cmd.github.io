The six weeks from Navratri to Bhai Dooj are the busiest buying season of the year for most Indian D2C brands. Customers are shopping for outfits, gifts, home décor, sweets and things for the new year, and every brand is talking at once. The brands that do well usually do not shout loudest; they plan early, stay on the right occasion at the right time, and keep their offers straight.

This is a practical plan you can follow from today.

## The 2026 festive calendar

| Navratri | 11–19 October | Pan-India, especially Gujarat and Maharashtra | Now |
| Durga Puja | 16–20 October | West Bengal, Assam, Odisha | Now |

| Karwa Chauth | 29 October | North India | 22 October |

Dates for lunar festivals can differ by a day between regions and almanacs. Check them against your customers' region before you print anything.

## Step 1: Decide what your brand is for this season

Do not try to show up for every festival. Pick the two or three occasions where your product is a natural fit:

- **Fashion and ethnic wear:** Navratri (outfits for each night), Durga Puja, Karwa Chauth, Diwali.
- **Jewellery and home goods:** Dhanteras (an auspicious day to buy), Diwali.
- **Food, sweets and gifting:** Diwali, Bhai Dooj, corporate gifting in the two weeks before Diwali.
- **Beauty and wellness:** Karwa Chauth, Diwali parties, the post-festive "reset".

Write one sentence for each chosen occasion: *"For Navratri, we are the brand for comfortable, hand-block-printed outfits you can dance in for nine nights."* Every post for that occasion should support that sentence.

## Step 2: Lock your offers before you write anything

Most festive mistakes are offer mistakes: a discount code that does not work, a price on a post that does not match the store, a "last day" that is not the last day.

Before you draft a single post, write down in one place:

- The exact offer for each occasion (percentage, flat amount, free gift or free shipping), and the products it covers.
- The code, if there is one, and when it starts and ends.
- Shipping cut-off dates for delivery before Diwali, by region.
- Your return and exchange policy for festive orders.

Put this on your website or in your store first, then build posts from it. Never the other way round.

## Step 3: A week-by-week plan

**Week of 5 October: warm-up.** Introduce the festive collection or range. Show the making, the materials or the people behind it. Start collecting interest: a "notify me" list, early-access sign-ups or a WhatsApp group.

**Week of 12 October: Navratri and Durga Puja.** Post daily during Navratri if your product fits: the colour of the day, styling ideas, customer photos (with permission). For Durga Puja, speak to Bengal-specific traditions only if you understand them.

**Week of 19 October: Dussehra, and the Diwali gifting push begins.** Dussehra is a "fresh start" day for new purchases. From here on, gifting is the main story: gift guides by budget and by recipient, corporate gifting enquiries, bundle offers.

**Week of 26 October: Karwa Chauth and Dhanteras build-up.** Karwa Chauth content is mostly North India; target it. Start Dhanteras messaging for anything that counts as a new purchase for the home.

**Week of 2 November: peak week.** Dhanteras on 6 November, Diwali on 8 November. Post shipping cut-offs clearly and repeatedly. Switch to "last chance" and "still time to gift" messages only when they are true. Have customer-service replies ready: order status, delivery dates, returns.

**Week of 9 November: after Diwali.** Bhai Dooj gifting, thank-you posts, and showing customers' festive photos. Move to a softer message: new-year essentials and "treat yourself" offers for people who spent the season buying for others.

## Step 4: Content that works in the festive rush

- **Product-first posts** with the real price on the image. Festive scrollers decide fast.
- **Carousels** for gift guides ("5 gifts under ₹1,000") and styling ("one kurta, three looks").
- **Short videos** showing the product in use, or being made. For small brands these often do better than polished ads.
- **Regional language posts** for regional festivals. A Bengali post for Durga Puja or a Gujarati one for Navratri shows that you know your customers.
- **Real customer proof only.** Share reviews and photos customers gave you permission to use. Never invent a testimonial.

## Step 5: Measure while it is happening, not after

Festive season is too short for a monthly report. Check every two or three days:

- Which posts drove **clicks to your store**, not just likes. Tag your links with UTM parameters so your analytics shows the source.
- Which **products** people are asking about in comments and messages.
- Which **channel** is bringing buyers, so you can move effort there for peak week.

Do more of what is working the same week. That is the whole advantage of a small, fast team.

## A quick checklist

- [ ] Two or three occasions chosen, with one sentence each
- [ ] Offers, codes, dates and shipping cut-offs written down and live on the store
- [ ] Posts planned week by week from 5 October to 15 November
- [ ] Gift guides and carousels ready before 19 October
- [ ] Regional-language posts for regional festivals
- [ ] Tracked links on every post
- [ ] Replies ready for order and delivery questions
