Most startup social media fails for a boring reason: there is no plan, so every post starts from a blank page, and blank pages lose to everything else on a founder's to-do list. This guide gives you a 30-day plan you can copy — pillars, cadence, templates and a simple way to measure it.

## Step 1: Pick three to four content pillars

Pillars are the recurring themes you post about. They keep your feed coherent and make "what should we post today?" a two-second question. Good pillars for early-stage startups:

1. **The problem** — the pain your customers live with, in their words.
2. **The product** — features, updates, how-tos and behind-the-scenes of building it.
3. **Proof** — customer stories, results, testimonials and screenshots (only real ones).
4. **The founder's point of view** — lessons, opinions and decisions, told honestly.

D2C brands often swap "the problem" for **craft or origin** (materials, makers, process) and add **styling or use cases**.

Write one sentence for each pillar describing what it is *for*. For example: "Proof posts show that people like our customers got results, so new visitors trust us."

## Step 2: Choose channels and a realistic cadence

Pick the two or three channels where your customers already spend time. A sustainable starting cadence:

| Channel | Posts per week | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | 3 | B2B, founder point of view, hiring, partnerships |
| X (Twitter) | 5 | Build in public, product updates, conversations |
| Instagram | 3–4 | D2C, visual products, behind the scenes |
| Community (Discord / Telegram) | 2 | Updates for your most engaged users |

It is better to post three times a week for a year than daily for two weeks.

## Step 3: The 30-day calendar

Map pillars to days so each week has a mix. Here is a template for a startup posting on LinkedIn and X.

### Week 1 — Introduce the problem and yourself

- **Mon (LinkedIn):** Why we started this company — the moment the problem became personal.
- **Tue (X):** Three signs you have [the problem]. A short thread.
- **Wed (LinkedIn):** What we tried before building [product], and why it did not work.
- **Thu (X):** A screenshot of the product with one sentence on what it does.
- **Fri (X):** A question to your audience about how they handle [the problem] today.

### Week 2 — Show the product

- **Mon (LinkedIn):** A 60-second walkthrough of the core workflow.
- **Tue (X):** One feature, one use case, one screenshot.
- **Wed (LinkedIn):** What we shipped this week and why.
- **Thu (X):** "Before / after" — the old way vs with the product.
- **Fri (X):** A small behind-the-scenes decision (naming, pricing, a design trade-off).

### Week 3 — Proof and people

- **Mon (LinkedIn):** A customer story — their situation, what changed, in their words (with permission).
- **Tue (X):** A lesson from a customer conversation this week.
- **Wed (LinkedIn):** A real number from your product or company, and what it taught you.
- **Thu (X):** A thank-you post tagging an early user or partner (again, with permission).
- **Fri (X):** A common objection and your honest answer.

### Week 4 — Point of view and a call to action

- **Mon (LinkedIn):** A contrarian opinion about your industry, argued well.
- **Tue (X):** A mistake you made this month and what you changed.
- **Wed (LinkedIn):** The roadmap: what's next and what you're deliberately not building.
- **Thu (X):** A clear call to action — try it, join the waitlist, book a demo.
- **Fri (X):** A recap thread of the month's best posts.

## Step 4: 20 post templates you can reuse

1. "Most [audience] think [common belief]. Here's what we've seen instead: …"
2. "We almost built [wrong thing]. Here's what changed our mind."
3. "[Number] things we learned from talking to [number] customers this month."
4. "How [customer type] can [outcome] without [painful thing]."
5. "This week we shipped [feature]. Here's the problem it solves: …"
6. "The one question we ask every new customer: …"
7. "Before [product]: [old workflow]. After: [new workflow]."
8. "We're [time] into building [company]. The honest scorecard: …"
9. "What we'd do differently if we started [company] today."
10. "Unpopular opinion: [opinion]. Here's why."
11. "A small detail most people miss in our product: …"
12. "Our pricing decision, explained."
13. "What [customer] said that made us rethink [thing]."
14. "The tool stack we run the company on."
15. "Three mistakes [audience] make with [topic]."
16. "Behind the scenes: how we [process]."
17. "The hardest bug / decision / week this month."
18. "If you're [audience] and struggling with [problem], try this: …"
19. "We're hiring / looking for partners — here's who we need."
20. "The month in one post: what shipped, what we learned, what's next."

## Step 5: Measure one thing per channel

Do not drown in analytics. For each channel pick one number that means "this is working" and review it weekly:

- **LinkedIn / X:** engagement rate (likes + comments + shares divided by impressions) and profile visits.
- **Instagram:** saves and shares per post — they signal content people want to come back to.
- **Everything:** clicks on a tracked link to your site or app.

Each week, look at your best and worst post. Ask: which pillar, which format, which hook? Then do more of the best and drop the worst. That single habit is worth more than any posting-time hack.

## Rules that keep a plan alive

- **Batch it.** Write a week of posts in one sitting, then schedule them.
- **Use only real facts.** Never invent numbers, testimonials or discount codes. It costs more trust than it earns.
- **Recycle what works.** A great post can come back in a new format a month later.
- **Protect the cadence in launch weeks.** That is exactly when a quiet feed hurts most.
