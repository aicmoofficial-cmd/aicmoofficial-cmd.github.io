For most Indian D2C brands, Instagram is the shop window, the customer-service desk and the brand story all at once. The good news: you do not need an agency to do it well. You need a clear position, a repeatable content mix, accurate product posts and a weekly routine you can actually keep.

## 1. Get your positioning into one sentence

Before you post, write this sentence and keep it where you can see it:

> We make **[product]** for **[specific customer]** who want **[outcome]**, unlike **[alternative]** which **[shortcoming]**.

For example: "We make hand-block-printed cotton kurtas for working women who want comfortable everyday ethnicwear, unlike fast-fashion prints that fade after a few washes."

Every caption, visual and reply should make that sentence more believable.

## 2. Build a content mix that sells without shouting

A feed of only "new arrival, shop now" posts gets ignored. A healthy mix for a D2C brand looks like this:

| Share of posts | Type | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| ~40% | **Product** | New drops, bestsellers, restocks, product details, sizing |
| ~25% | **Craft and origin** | Materials, makers, process, why it costs what it costs |
| ~20% | **Styling and use** | How to wear or use it, occasions, combinations |
| ~15% | **Community and proof** | Customer photos (with permission), reviews, questions answered |

The non-product posts are what make the product posts work: they give people a reason to care about the product before they see the price.

## 3. Make product posts that convert

A product post has one job: make the right person tap through to the product. What helps:

- **Real photos of the actual product.** Clean background, true colour, the detail that makes it special.
- **Name and price on the image.** People scroll past posts that make them hunt for the price.
- **One specific benefit in the first line** of the caption, not the brand's mission statement.
- **Accurate information.** The price, sizes and availability must match your store *today*. A wrong price or a discount code that does not exist creates angry DMs and refunds.
- **A clear next step.** "Tap the product tag", "link in bio" or "DM us your size".

## 4. Use carousels and captions for depth

Carousels let you tell a small story: the product, a close-up of the fabric or finish, how it is made, how it looks in use, and the price. Captions carry the details people ask about in DMs — fit, material, care, delivery time — so answer them before they are asked.

Hashtags matter far less than they used to. Use a handful that describe the product and category precisely, rather than thirty generic ones.

## 5. A weekly routine you can keep

The routine matters more than any single post. A realistic one for a founder:

- **Monday (30 minutes):** plan the week — which products, which stories, which days.
- **Tuesday (60 minutes):** create and schedule all posts for the week in one batch.
- **Daily (10 minutes):** reply to comments and DMs. Speed here builds trust and sales.
- **Friday (15 minutes):** look at the week's numbers and note one thing to repeat and one to drop.

Three or four good posts a week, every week, beats a burst of daily posting followed by silence.

## 6. Measure what matters

Instagram gives you plenty of numbers. For a D2C brand, watch:

- **Saves and shares** per post — they show content people value enough to keep or send to a friend.
- **Reach** — are new people seeing you, or only existing followers?
- **Profile visits and link taps** — the bridge from content to your store.
- **Sales from Instagram** in your store analytics, using tracked links so you know which posts led to orders.

Compare posts within the same format; a carousel and a single image are different games.
